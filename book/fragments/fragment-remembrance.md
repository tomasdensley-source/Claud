# Fragment of the Old Covenant — Remembrance

The faithful were not told merely to remember. They were taught to remember actively.

A name stored in a closed book was sleeping, not witnessing. A statue unseen in a field was stone, not memory. A story repeated inaccurately could preserve a shape while erasing the person who had lived inside it.

Therefore the rites required consciousness. A name was spoken. A true act was attached to it. Another person received the account and corrected it if necessary. The dead were not made flattering. Accuracy was a form of love because it allowed the remembered person to remain distinct rather than dissolve into usefulness.

The ancient rule said: *No person shall depend upon one witness alone.*

It was first understood as protection against error. Later generations learned that it also protected against disappearance.

Death ended a life but did not deny that the life had been.

Departure continued a life elsewhere.

Erasure removed the life from memory, record, cause, and grief until the world reorganized around the claim that no one had been lost.

The covenant feared erasure more than death.

Its prayer was not, *Let me live forever.*

It was, *Let me remain true in the memory of creation, and if creation forgets, let there be a witness deeper than creation.*
