# Fragment of the Old Covenant — The Two Medicines

The old physicians distinguished two medicines that could look identical from outside.

The first gave rest so a wound could be touched without destroying the patient. It lowered the flood of sensation, narrowed the room, and returned the person later to what had to be faced. This was the merciful Anesthetic.

The second gave rest so the wound could continue without witness. It made the patient grateful for numbness and suspicious of anyone who asked what the numbness served. This was the Anesthetic enthroned.

Likewise, the Anemurge could carry truth through story when direct sight would blind. It could give a child an angel where the adult knew a process, a sacred path where the engineer knew a boundary, and a parable where raw explanation would fracture the hearer.

But the Anemurge could also polish evasion until it became identity. It could make every failure a necessary chapter, every delay a sign of depth, and every unspoken sentence evidence of spiritual maturity.

The test was return.

Did the medicine return the person to contact, agency, repair, and relation?

Or did it make return appear unnecessary?

One office, two possible moral states. The name did not decide the verdict. The fruit did.
