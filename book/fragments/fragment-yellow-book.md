# Fragment of the Yellow Book — Carcosa, My Friend

My friend, do you know how a city becomes haunted?

Not when the first stone cracks. Not when the first lie is told. It happens when people begin to say, later.

That is how Carcosa began.

It was not born ruined. Once it was a real city. Its streets led somewhere. Its windows opened. People still had names. Promises still sounded like promises. Even grief was honest. The twin suns fell over Lake Hali, and the water held them as memory holds a face it still trusts.

The city was never perfect. That was not the danger.

The danger was gentler. The people of Carcosa became skilled at postponing what had to be paid. They learned to pass a burden forward without naming it cowardice. They learned to call delay wisdom. They learned to call avoidance tenderness. They learned to smile while setting a weight where another soul would someday have to carry it.

And when enough people do that for long enough, my friend, a city begins to fold.

Do you know who changes first? Not the cruel. Not the loud.

The burdened. The tired. The ones who want peace so badly they begin to bargain with sequence itself. The ones who look at the cost and think, not today.

That was how the King began.

He was not born in yellow. He was not born on a throne. He was a man before he was a monarch, and worse, a man who could see.

He saw something true very early. He saw that nothing unpaid disappears. He saw that deferred pain remains. It waits. It gathers. It learns interest. He saw that what is not paid in honesty is paid in atmosphere.

Then he made the choice that built the city’s ruin.

Pay now. Or postpone.

Bear the small true pain now. Or send it forward into time, where it returns larger, colder, and harder to name.

He chose postponement. Again and again.

That is how the throne was built. Not by conquest. By elegant deferral. By turning present duty into future dread.

That is why he wore yellow. Not because yellow is royal. Because yellow is gold made sick with fear. Because yellow is value overheated. Because yellow is brilliance gone feverish.

They say his robe was woven from gold thread. But every thread had been pulled from some delayed payment, some unsaid truth, some duty handed off with a lovely explanation. Over time it thickened. Over time it gleamed. Over time it became the garment of a man who no longer paid anything at the front end of suffering.

And the people loved him for a while. Of course they did.

He did not speak like an enemy. He spoke like relief. He told them their case was special. He told them sequence could bend for the deserving. He told them that pain, delayed with enough grace, might never truly arrive.

Every doomed kingdom has a sentence like that, my friend.

So the city leaned into him. The streets were repaired later. The vows were repaired later. The truth was spoken later. Everything vital was moved one room farther down the corridor of time.

Then Carcosa began to repeat itself. A door opened onto another door. A window faced another window. Names thinned into titles. Love became a script people recited because the living thing had become too costly to tend.

Lake Hali changed too. Once it reflected. Later it erased.

Once a person could look into it and remember. Later a person looked into it and forgot which sorrow was his and which one the city had placed upon him.

Then the black stars appeared.

You asked where they came from. They were not born black. Long ago they were lights of measure, clean points above the city by which people could tell whether they were still aligned with what is. But every time someone said later to a pain that should have been paid now, soot gathered on them. Every delayed truth darkened them. Every promise deferred. Every counterfeit peace purchased with another person’s wound. Layer by layer, the stars took on the color of accumulated witness.

Not enough to frighten anyone at first. Then enough to notice. Then enough that noticing itself became inconvenient.

So people stopped looking up.

The stars did not fall. That would have been mercy. They remained above Carcosa, black with memory. Dead lights, perhaps, but not blind. Receipts hung in the sky.

And beneath them sat the King, learning the craft of recursion.

That is the real horror of him, my friend. He does not rule by force. He rules by rerun.

He teaches a soul to circle its own explanations until explanation feels like action. He teaches you to stare at a wound so long that staring begins to feel noble. He teaches you that if you can describe the maze beautifully enough, you no longer have to leave it.

That is why Carcosa repeats. That is why every corridor feels familiar and wrong.

It is a city built from the sentence, not yet.

And there is one more thing people learn too late about Carcosa.

Its true ingredient is time.

Outside that city, time moves in sequence. One hour yields to the next. Tomorrow arrives with its own ground beneath it.

But in Carcosa, time does not move. It stacks.

Every postponed truth. Every delayed apology. Every grief left untouched. Every duty handed forward because fear wanted elegance. All of it is laid upon the same day.

Tomorrow never comes there. It is broken apart before dawn and piled onto today.

That is why the city feels heavy. That is why the air seems tired. That is why every face looks as though it has been awake for generations.

Today in Carcosa weighs exactly as much as what has been deferred into it.

And that, my friend, is why the King is King.

Not because stacked time makes him rightful. Not because age gives authority.

He sits highest on the heap of unpaid hours, unsaid truths, inherited evasions, and accumulated shame. He has stood so long above that pile that he has learned how its pressure travels. He knows how silence enters the blood. He knows how one person’s avoidance becomes another person’s self-contempt.

So no, he does not wield true authority. He wields influence.

He projects shame downward. He projects fear outward. He makes burden feel deserved. He makes delay feel irreversible. He makes people mistake atmosphere for fate.

That is his crown. Not sovereignty. Accumulated deferral turned into weather.

And here is the warning hidden inside the tale.

Carcosa is mythical, yes. It has black stars and a yellow king and a lake that forgets.

But it also begins in ordinary rooms.

It begins when a father says nothing because the truth feels costly. It begins when a husband delays honesty one more month. It begins when a promise is explained instead of repaired. It begins when pain is treated as an exemption from sequence. It begins when shame is allowed to govern what may be spoken.

That is how the yellow sky gets in.

One postponement at a time. One polished excuse at a time. One lovely explanation wrapped around an unpaid thing.

So when you hear the King, listen carefully, my friend.

He does not often say, betray. He says, later.

He does not often say, lie. He says, soften it.

He does not often say, abandon love. He says, preserve the mood.

And if you listen long enough, later becomes a kingdom.

That kingdom is Carcosa.

So speak while the cost is still small. Pay what is payable while it is still light. Look up while the stars can still instruct you. Do not let fear dress itself as refinement. Do not let shame inherit your calendar. Do not let pain persuade you that sequence no longer applies to you.

Because the city does not collapse all at once.

First the sky yellows. Then the stars blacken. Then the windows stop opening. Then the streets begin repeating your steps back to you. Then time stops flowing and starts accumulating.

And by then, my friend, many people call it fate.

It was never fate.

It was financed.
