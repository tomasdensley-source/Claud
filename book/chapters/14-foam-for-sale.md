# Chapter 14 — Foam for Sale

*Foam on the surface of the work*

The Foam Sellers arrived during the year of the water debate. Eiren hated the commerce and secretly enjoyed seeing his language everywhere.

Foam spread because it was pleasant. The colored ribbons were handsome. The keeper songs made children laugh. A person could purchase a bronze Glaw and feel enlisted without repairing anything. The market sold tiny unhinged doors as charms against coercion, though every seller demanded payment before allowing a customer to leave.

The contradiction embarrassed him enough that he called the merchants shallow rather than examining his pleasure. Torren understood that pleasure better than he did.

“You wanted the House to change the town,” Torren said. “Now the town uses it and you complain they use it incorrectly.”

“They use the symbols without the work.”

Torren turned a bronze charm between his fingers. “People always do. That is what symbols are for.”

“No. A symbol is a handle. It should return the hand to the weight.”

Torren lifted a small brass Keeper of Report from a stall. “Most people prefer a handle that proves they have already lifted it.”

He was not defending the foam. He was studying it.

Within months his public meetings adopted the House’s language while reversing its discipline. He spoke of witness but repeated rumor. He demanded corrigibility from elders while treating his own suspicion as exempt. He called every inherited boundary a Bastion and every request for patience an Anesthetic. The words were not false. They had simply been relieved of the obligations that made them true.

Eiren saw his own work in the yellow movement. The House had taught Verrin to question authority without teaching the difference between questioning a throne and desiring one.

Torren had charm, a good memory for grievance, and the rare ability to make a person feel brave for choosing the easiest interpretation. He staged public debates in which every elder sounded frightened and every doubt sounded noble. He did not say the religion was false. He said truth should survive ridicule if it deserved obedience.

That sentence was fair.

Then he arranged the ridicule so nothing else could survive it.

The old god’s broken throne became a stage. Torren sat there during performances, crowned in yellow cloth, reading passages from the covenant in the voice of a fool.

The crowds laughed.

Each performance was followed by a new irregularity.

A fountain poured upward for three breaths. A child returned home with a sister no one else remembered. The eastern street repeated one doorway seven times. People blamed drink, heat, attention, and keepers eager to preserve authority.

The more the world failed, the more Torren’s movement grew. Every failure of ordinary reality became proof that the guardians of reality were lying.

Eiren confronted him after a performance.

“You are teaching people to break the shelter before they know what weather it holds back.”

Torren removed the yellow crown. “You sound exactly like every jailer who ever called a wall mercy.”

“Some walls are prisons. Some are skin.”

Torren rolled the crown into a narrow band. “And the person inside never gets to decide which?”

Eiren had no quick answer.

Torren smiled. “There. The House has no drawer for me.”

“It does. The drawer is called contradiction.”

“No. The drawer is called later. You will study me until the field is gone.”

Eiren returned to the One House and found three reports on Torren’s movement, two maps of the field, and no action entered in the live register.
