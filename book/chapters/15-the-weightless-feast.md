# Chapter 15 — The Weightless Feast

*The feast that owed no lesson*

Mara declared an archive Sabbath.

The feast began before sunset and outlasted the lamps. Because bodies in Verrin required little, abundance did not mean excess so much as variety: five kinds of bread, bowls of herbs, honey poured over cold pears, roasted river fish, cream, wine diluted with spring water, and berries children crushed between their fingers to paint one another purple.

Mara had arranged no program. That omission made several keepers nervous. Sister Ilya asked three times when the names would be called. Hadrin wanted to know who would close the meal. Senn wondered whether the unrecorded hour needed witnesses proving that it had occurred.

“This is why I took your notebooks,” Mara said.

She and Eiren walked beyond the tables where orchard grass cooled under their feet. Music drifted through the leaves, losing rhythm with distance.

“Do you love the House?” she asked.

“Yes.”

“More than me?”

He tried to reject the comparison. “They are not the same kind of love.”

“That was not the question.”

The honest answer changed from day to day, which was why he had avoided asking it.

“Sometimes,” he said.

Mara nodded. The admission hurt her less than the polished answer would have.

“Then do not promise me it will never happen again. Promise me a sign I can use when you disappear into it.”

They chose a simple sentence: You are standing outside the hour. It required no argument. When Mara spoke it, Eiren would put the instrument down.

That was the only law written during the feast, and it was written the next morning with both their names beneath it.

For an hour, perhaps two, Eiren ceased observing the life he was living. He lived it.

Nothing was learned.

No principle was improved.

No wound became a gift.

The evening did not justify itself by increasing productivity the next morning. It was good without becoming weight.

Near midnight, Sister Ilya began the Calling of Names. Mara stopped her.

“Not tonight.”

Ilya looked shocked. “Remembrance is never suspended.”

“Then remember us by being here.”

There was a long silence.

Sister Ilya set down the bowl.

That night he and Mara slept beneath the orchard trees.

No record was made.

The world did not punish them for the omission.
