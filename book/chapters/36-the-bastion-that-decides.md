# Chapter 36 — The Bastion That Decides

*The gate that decides who may need it*

Councilor Odran had become the Bastion.

He had not become cruel. He had become indispensable, which is more dangerous. During the year of unstable doors, he organized food, marked safe roads, rebuilt damaged houses, and prevented panic from becoming famine. People trusted him because he had carried weight while others argued.

Now he used that trust to forbid revision.

The waterworks would continue. The rites would be permitted as private comfort but not allowed to interfere with civic planning. Reports of changed roads were classified as stress. Missing names were transferred to the House for study. Public discussion of the Wall required council approval.

“I kept the gate closed so people would not have to decide while afraid,” Odran told Eiren.

“The gate belongs to the people who must live behind it.”

“They are exhausted.”

“Then let them answer exhausted. Do not confuse fatigue with surrender.”

Eiren put both hands on the table. “Consent given under terror can authorize an emergency. It does not authorize forever.”

Odran looked past him. “Who are you?”

The question was not rhetorical.

Eiren felt the correction move through the room. Odran remembered the One House, the field, the missing year, Mara, Senn, and Neris. He did not remember Eiren.

Mara stepped forward. “He built the House.”

“The House had many builders.”

“He sat on the throne.”

Odran’s face changed. Blasphemy remained easier to remember than a person.

Eiren placed the Bronze Guard’s two questions on the council table.

“What changed in the meaning? How was the change verified?”

Odran read them slowly.

The waterworks had been proposed as a means of cleaning water. It had become a declaration that the old covenant possessed no public authority. The project had changed meaning. No audit had recorded the change.

“Do you ask me to abandon clean water for a ruin?” Odran said.

“No. I ask you to stop treating the choice as one between usefulness and reverence. The old conduit can carry the new works if we repair what we broke.”

“And if your religion is wrong?”

“Then the design still preserves the pillar network and gives the town clean water.”

“And if you are wrong about the danger?”

“Then I have spent my remaining name on an unnecessary repair.”

Odran stared at him.

“Your remaining what?”

The correction had already taken the sentence.

Odran read the two questions again. He had spent forty years being a man who could not walk away from a question in bronze.

“The hearing,” he said. “In the field. All of it, and everyone, and I will not close it when it turns against me.”

The Bastion opened one gate and remained standing.
