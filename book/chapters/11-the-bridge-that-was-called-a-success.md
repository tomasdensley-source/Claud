# Chapter 11 — The Bridge That Was Called a Success

*The report that survived the river*

Before Neris was a keeper she was a clerk in the works office at Anselm Ford, and the works office at Anselm Ford was one room with a stove that smoked, and her job was to copy other people’s sentences without improving them.

The bridge was a timber trestle across the Anselm, built in a summer, opened in a summer, and paid for by a levy on the dyers of three villages. It carried carts. It carried the flock down in autumn. For two years it carried everything anyone put on it, and then in the third spring the river came up brown and fast with a winter’s worth of the mountain in it, and the middle span went.

Four people were on it. Three came out downstream at the willow bend, cold and shouting and alive. The fourth was a girl of eleven named Sera Loam who had been sent to the far bank to fetch her father, and she did not come out at the willow bend, and she did not come out at the mill, and on the fourth day they found one shoe.

Neris was nineteen. She was given the report to copy.

The report said that the Anselm crossing had performed as a successful experiment in rapid timber construction, with unexpected outcomes arising from an exceptional hydrological event, and that the methods developed would inform future works.

She read the sentence four times. Nothing in it was a lie. The bridge had been an experiment. The flood had been exceptional. The methods would in fact inform future works, and did.

She read it a fifth time and understood that she was holding a machine for making a drowned girl into a lesson, and that the machine ran on true sentences, and that this was why nobody would ever be able to stop it by pointing out a lie.

She copied it. That was her work and she did it, and she never once claimed otherwise, and it was the part of the story she told first.

Then she took a second sheet and copied it again, and beneath it she wrote a line of her own:

This report does not record that the works office was warned in writing about the middle span in the autumn before the flood, or that the warning was filed, or that the man who filed it is the man who wrote the words successful experiment.

She put the second sheet in the same box as the first. She did not send it anywhere. She did not show it to anyone for eleven years.

What she carried into the House was that a report can remain true in every line and still be corrupted by what it leaves out.

When Eiren offered her the office of Report she asked one question before she answered.

“If I record that the House failed, and the House is right about everything else, do you take it out?”

“No.”

“If I record that you failed?”

“Especially then.”

She said she would think about it, and she thought about it for six days, and on the seventh she came to the House with a wooden box under her arm and put it on the table and opened it, and inside were two sheets of paper in the same hand, and she said, “This is what I am for.”

Sera Loam’s name is on the first stone of the Cold Hall. It was Neris who put it there, and she put it there before she put anything of her own.
