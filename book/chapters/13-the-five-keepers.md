# Chapter 13 — The Five Keepers

*The five offices gather around the living record*

Eiren appointed four keepers and set a fifth chair at the table.

The four keepers were people before they were offices.

Senn became Keeper of Convergence because he mistrusted agreement that arrived too quickly. Neris became Keeper of Report because she had once watched a failed bridge described as a successful experiment with unexpected outcomes. Hadrin accepted Compression because a carpenter knows the difference between removing waste and cutting through a load-bearing joint. Sister Ilya kept Home because she believed every living claim needed one place where a person could find its current form without searching a maze. Mara refused an office and therefore became, unofficially, the person who asked whether the offices had mistaken the House for the world.

Their first council was noisy. Senn wanted every witness path preserved in full. Hadrin said no living person could use an archive that required three days to answer a practical question. Ilya wanted one canonical wording. Neris insisted the rejected wordings remain visible. Eiren tried to solve the dispute by drawing a diagram and discovered that the diagram reproduced his own preferences.

Mara watched from the window.

“Who judges the keepers?” she asked.

“Each other,” Eiren replied.

“That is one source wearing four hats.”

The sentence irritated all of them and entered the minutes.

They established an outside chair that rotated among people who did not work in the House. Its occupant’s turn ended with the meeting. The first was a baker. She listened to two hours of argument about semantic lineage and said, “I still cannot tell which bread order is current.”

The meeting ended. The filing system changed.

The five offices were then written into the House’s public discipline.

The Keeper of Home ensured that every living claim had one place where its current form could be found.

The Keeper of Meaning asked what had actually changed when a word, category, or doctrine was revised.

The Keeper of Convergence kept independent roads apart until their meeting had been earned.

The Keeper of Compression shortened without blurring, refusing summaries that made unlike things appear identical.

The Keeper of Report required uncertainty, failure, cost, and correction to remain visible.

That left the fifth office.

Eiren wrote the Keeper of Meaning into the discipline and then did not fill the chair. He set it at the table with the others, turned slightly outward, and left it there.

Senn thought it was an oversight and said so twice.

“Who asks what changed, then?” he said. “Somebody has to ask.”

“Everyone asks. That is the point of the seat. If one person owns the question, the rest of you will stop carrying it, and you will bow to whoever is sitting there, and within a year the meaning of a word will be whatever that person had for breakfast.”

“Then why keep the chair at all?”

“Because a table with four chairs looks finished.”

Ilya laughed, which she rarely did in council, and the sound surprised everyone including her.

The fifth chair was not the outside chair. The outside chair rotated; this one remained at the table, an office without an owner. Nor was it a throne awaiting a claimant. It was a working chair.

Visitors sat in it and answered what they had come to change. Every council began that way. It became the House’s only successful ritual.

The people of Verrin called them the Five Keepers and soon treated the offices as powers.

Visitors bowed to the Keeper of Home before filing contradictory claims in three different rooms. Merchants purchased amulets bearing the Keeper of Meaning while changing labels without recording the change. Scholars invoked Convergence when repeating one another. Scribes praised Compression and removed every inconvenient distinction. Officials quoted Honest Report in reports from which every failed measure had been deleted.

The keepers became popular at the exact moment their work became optional.

Senn called this foam.

“Foam is not false,” he explained. “It is surface activity detached from semantic labor. It looks busy because emptiness needs decoration.”

The market began selling colored ribbons for each keeper. Children traded miniature jars. A baker shaped bread into five little faces. The council proposed an annual parade.

Eiren objected.

The council accused him of disliking his own success.

Mara said, “They have found a way to admire the instrument without submitting to it. That is what people do with every demanding truth.”

Eiren established the Bronze Guard at the House’s entrance. The guard was not a person at first, but two questions carved into metal:

**WHAT CHANGED IN THE MEANING?**

**HOW WAS THE CHANGE VERIFIED?**

Every new doctrine had to pass between them.

Within a month, someone began selling bronze copies of the questions as desk ornaments.
