# Chapter 9 — The Keeper of Jars

*The two springs and the single source*

Senn kept evidence in jars because he distrusted shelves.

His workroom stood beneath the archive. The walls were lined with thousands of vessels, each marked with a place, a date, and the name of the person who had carried it in.

“A claim needs one home,” Senn told Eiren on the first day of his apprenticeship. “Its warrants need many roads. Confuse the roads, and an echo becomes a crowd.”

He placed three jars on the table.

The first contained water clear as air. The second contained water with a reddish stain. The third was empty.

“Two springs feed the lower fountain,” Senn said. “One from the east hill and one from under the old court. The fountain tastes of iron. Which spring carries it?”

Eiren examined the seals. “The red one.”

“That is the answer a color gives. Now give the answer the roads give.”

They walked to the eastern spring and drew water in Eiren’s presence. They followed the buried conduit near the throne and drew from the second. They asked the dyers whether pigments had entered either channel. They questioned a shepherd who had seen a rusted tool fall into the fountain basin.

The reddish jar proved to have been tinted accidentally by Senn’s own spoon.

Color rose above Senn’s collar. He took up the label and added his own error to it: Reddish stain introduced by Senn’s spoon during handling.

The iron taste came from the fallen tool.

“The brightest witness was created by the instrument,” Senn said. “Remember that. A method can manufacture the evidence by which it later congratulates itself.”

Shelves allowed one thing to lean against another until resemblance became kinship. A jar kept its contents honest. A pinch of white soil from the north road remained north-road soil even when placed beside ash from the chapel fire. A sliver of blue glass from a vanished window did not become proof of the sea merely because the two shared a color.

Senn’s jars were not beautiful. Many had chipped rims, mismatched lids, or old labels crossed through three times. Senn believed cleanliness could become another way of making evidence look more certain than it was.

He taught Eiren to write the journey before the conclusion. Who carried the object? Who touched it? What did each person expect to find? Which tool had been used? What failed? What did not get measured? A report without these things was an answer without a road.

One afternoon Torren brought in a flake of yellow stone from the throne and demanded that it be tested. Senn weighed it, scraped it, placed it in water, held it to flame, and compared it with pigment from the dye houses.

“Well?” Torren asked.

“It contains iron, lime, and a compound I cannot name.”

Torren held the flake to the light.

“Is it alive?”

“That is not a question the jar can answer.”

“Does it prove the god approved?”

“No.”

“Does it prove he did not?”

“No.”

Torren set the flake beside the jar and smiled at the gathered apprentices. “Then the archive has learned nothing.”

Senn pushed the jar toward him. “It has learned exactly what it learned. Your disappointment does not reduce the finding.”

After Torren left, Eiren asked whether there was any use in preserving evidence that settled so little.

“The little is what keeps the large from lying,” Senn said. “A person who cannot endure a narrow answer will eventually manufacture a wide one.”

He made Eiren relabel the jar three times until the wording neither implied miracle nor dismissed it.

Eiren understood why the archive had three chambers.

The Live House held claims still governing conduct.

The Evidence Cellar held witnesses, sources, objects, and competing accounts.

The Cold Hall held what had once been believed and later corrected. Nothing in the Cold Hall was mocked. Each failed law stood beside the date of its revision and the evidence that had required the change.

“Why keep errors?” Eiren asked.

“So the House cannot pretend it was born wise.”

He thought of the road with six turns and the portrait with a missing man. “What if the evidence changes after it is stored?”

Senn looked at him for a long time.

“Then you need witnesses who do not all sleep in the same world.”
