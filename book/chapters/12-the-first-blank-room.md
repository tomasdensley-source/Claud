# Chapter 12 — The First Blank Room

In the tenth year of the House, the House That Remembered began with a practical problem. Eiren forgot what he had decided.

The first people to use the One House were not philosophers. They were tradespeople who needed to remember what had been promised. A mason stored the date on which a wall had begun to lean. A mother preserved the exact words of a healer who later denied giving them. Two brothers recorded their different memories of a debt rather than forcing one account to win by volume.

The House became useful because it did not require agreement before preservation. It could hold two testimonies beside one another and mark the conflict without pretending the conflict itself was clarity.

Eiren loved the building more than was safe. He rose before dawn to check the live register. He began hearing ordinary conversation in headings. Mara noticed first.

At breakfast she said, “I dreamed you built a room so complete that nobody could enter without becoming a document.”

Eiren reached for his pencil.

She closed her hand over his.

“That,” she said.

He laughed, then saw that she was not teasing.

“The House keeps me from losing what matters.”

“It also lets you postpone being changed by what matters.”

“How?”

“You turn the moment into material. Then you can visit the material later, when it is less likely to answer back.”

The accusation felt unfair because it was accurate in a way Eiren had not yet learned to distinguish from cruelty. He created the first unrecorded hour that evening. He lasted twelve minutes before writing down why unrecorded hours might be necessary.

Mara took the page, folded it, and walked him to the empty drawer beside the archive wing door.

“What category is that?” he asked.

“A thing the House must not use yet.”

She placed the page in the drawer and kept her palm on it while Eiren carved a warning into the door:

An external organ that preserves life may still feed upon the life it preserves.

Then Mara took the knife and added below it, in smaller letters:

Some hours survive by remaining unrecorded.
