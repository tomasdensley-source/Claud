# Chapter 8 — The Miracle of the Lost Child

*The keepers carry the names through the town*

The first miracle Eiren witnessed was small enough to be mistaken for persistence.

Pela’s mother, Sava, refused to let the search become a spectacle. She stood beside Sister Ilya and corrected every memory that made the child sweeter than she was.

“She does not merely dislike onions,” Sava said. “She hides them under other people’s bread. She sings before sleep because she believes the song keeps the ceiling from falling. She lies when frightened and tells the truth five minutes later.”

Each correction gave the missing girl more shape. The crowd’s first memories had been generic: kind, bright, beloved. Sava forced them toward the particular. Pela had a crooked lower tooth. She called rain ’sky-river.’ She could not tie her left shoe.

When the door appeared, it did not blaze. A seam darkened in the stone and acquired depth. Someone near Eiren gasped, but Sister Ilya raised one finger and the sound stopped. The keepers had long taught that astonishment should not become possession. A miracle was not made more real by turning it into public property before the person inside it had come home.

Eiren entered behind Sava. The corridor smelled faintly of dust and pear skins. Its repeating wallpaper looked almost ordinary until one noticed that every pear had the same bruise. Pela sat where the corridor turned, though from the market wall there should have been no room for a turn.

On the way out, Eiren glanced back. The hall continued beyond the place where they had found her. Its lights hummed without flame. Far away, something moved across the passage and did not resemble an animal.

Sister Ilya pulled him through before he could look longer.

“Curiosity is not always courage,” she said.

“What was down there?”

“Something the rite was not asking you to meet.”

That night he added Pela’s name to his book with seven memories beneath it.

On the facing page, the blank token had left a circular stain.

Inside the stain appeared a sentence in handwriting he did not recognize:

*No person shall depend upon one witness alone.*
