# Chapter 20 — The Reviser

*Correction waits at the beloved door*

Neris, the youngest Keeper of Report, carried chalk into the Live House.

Neris did not argue against the House from outside it. She used its own rules and therefore exposed how selectively those rules had been applied.

She began with the word witness. In early documents it meant a person whose distinct path could be traced. Later, the House had used it for any repeated formulation inside its own books. Neris drew a line through the later usage and wrote: Echoes are not additional witnesses, even when the echo is beautiful.

The keepers objected less to the correction than to the chalk. It made revision visible to visitors.

“A clean copy can be made after the review,” Hadrin said.

“Why?” Neris asked.

“So people know what the House teaches.”

“They should also know it once taught something else.”

She opened the Cold Hall, a room where superseded claims remained dated, sourced, and connected to the reasons they had changed. The room embarrassed Eiren. It also made the House more trustworthy in a way no statement of humility ever had.

A young visitor stood before a wall of corrections and asked, “Which one is the real House?”

Neris answered, “The one that includes the record of having been wrong.”

Most visitors saw that the House had not fallen when a sentence changed. For the first time, Eiren imagined inheritance as a structure whose descendants could see the cuts.

The Live House contained statements still governing the community. They were written on dark boards in careful gold letters: The Wall protects the faithful. The First Ones crossed without sleeping. No witness may be erased while another bears the name. The throne must remain empty. The covenant cannot contradict itself.

Neris placed a white mark beside the last sentence.

“What are you doing?” Sister Ilya asked.

“Recording a contradiction.”

“The covenant is whole.”

“Then the contradiction is ours.”

She had found two rites that could not both be obeyed during the demolition. One commanded the town to preserve the First One’s court. Another commanded the council to keep water clean and accessible. The keepers had solved the conflict by pretending one rite was symbolic and the other practical.

Neris refused the distinction.

“Both are practical,” she said. “Or neither is covenant.”

Eiren watched the elders surround her. They loved correction in the Cold Hall, where it belonged safely to the past. Correction in the Live House felt like vandalism.

“Nothing here was born canonical,” Neris said. “Every line entered through a hand. Every hand could be wrong.”

She wrote the date beside her chalk mark.

The act did not destroy the House. It revealed what the House had built.

A museum of courage and a government of fear.

Privately, Eiren resented the place she had marked. The sentence about the covenant’s coherence had been his own.

That resentment taught him more than agreement would have.

Eiren supported her publicly.

He established the architect’s audit. Every instrument the House applied to others would be applied first to the House itself. Its graph could not certify the architecture that generated it. Its categories could not declare every unclassified truth malformed. Its founder could not exempt a beloved sentence from chalk.

That evening, Mara returned to the unhinged eastern door.

“You finally built an exit,” she said.

“Neris built it. I only stopped calling the opening damage.”

After Mara left, Ilya remained before the marked board. She performed the Calling of Names without the bowl and addressed what the books could not contain.

“You beyond the Wall, these names are not totals; we speak them because each belongs before you.
Do not let our shelves counterfeit communion; receive us where no archive can.”
