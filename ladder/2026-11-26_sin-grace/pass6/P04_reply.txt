1. **DEMONSTRATED.** The two differ in direction over time rather than in any single instant, so a momentary likeness does not erase the difference.

2. **DEMONSTRATED.** The first act is a discrete deed, while remaining turned away is a continuing stance toward it; each can be present without the other being exhausted.

3. **DEMONSTRATED.** Knowing persistence in wrong, as in defiance or akrasia, shows that turning away does not need ignorance or forgetting.

4. **FALSE AS STATED.** Knowledge can stay present during moments of turning back (see 1) or of incapacity, so not every later moment is a moment of sin.

5. **DEMONSTRATED.** One can judge an act to be a wrong while not knowing or denying that it was one's own, so recognizing its character and owning it come apart.

6. **DEMONSTRATED.** A defiant or despairing admission ("yes, it was wrong, and I did it") can sit alongside a refusal to turn toward the work.

7. **DEMONSTRATED.** Apology, restitution, amends, and changed conduct are deeds toward a past act that leave the act itself unchanged.

8. **CONTESTED.** It is disputed whether bearing consequences is a deed of facing at all, or only passive endurance that might be bare submission.

9. **DEMONSTRATED.** This follows from 5: seeing an act's character accurately does not require recognizing it as one's own.

10. **CONTESTED.** Some hold that attention and deed are separate registers; others, such as Murdoch, hold that attention that is truly complete already moves the will toward action.

11. **DEMONSTRATED.** Given 7, deeds toward a past work are available, so repentance can reach them as well as reaching acknowledgment.

12. **CONTESTED.** Cases where no deed is possible, such as deathbed contrition, divide views on whether deedless contrition is complete or deficient.

13. **CONTESTED.** Whether any part of a facing can be received rather than done depends on disputed views of grace, forgiveness, and moral luck.

14. **CONTESTED.** The split is coherent but depends on 13 and on a particular division between gift and achievement that rival accounts reject.
