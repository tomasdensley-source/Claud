| Change from REV-30 | Receipt |
|---|---|

Named losses: repentance as the turning toward grace (the author's ruling O-0116 in part: "turning towards God's grace"); "repentance contains sin's contrary only once it comes to that standing"; "though not every part of oneself can be seen" (its bound is carried by "as far as these can be seen").

| ID | Exact sentence |
|---|---|
| REV-31 | Sin is remaining turned away, after understanding, from what one has made, done or left undone as its author. Its contrary is to stand turned toward that work, rather than away from it, as one's own and for what it is, right or wrong, in acknowledgment and in the deeds it rightly calls for, as far as these can be seen; attention alone never amounts to that standing. Grace is the free giving of that standing, though one can also come to it with no one giving it. Repentance toward God is the return toward God, and away from one's own turning from His call or His work; whether sought or found, it is not fulfilled while it still refuses to turn toward what God has made, as God's work, or toward oneself within it as a whole, as far as these can be seen. |
