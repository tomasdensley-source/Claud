The concept is **facing**: the stance of standing toward something as it really is, and keeping that stance. You use it everywhere, but you've never said what it is. You've only said what it isn't.

**Your terms all rest on it:**
- **Sin** is remaining turned away from the work of one's own hand.
- **Sin's contrary** is to "stand facing that work."
- **Grace** is "the free giving of that facing."
- **Repentance** is turning toward that grace. It is complete only "once it arrives at that facing."
- **Turning** is the movement of the face.
- **Hand** is what gets faced, the work as one's own.
- **Naming** is facing done in speech, the acknowledgment half of your thesis. Carcosa, "postponed declaration," is a refusal to face that has become a place.
- **Charity** is what you put at the hinge of the Cornerstone Choice, holding the No to the false world and the Yes to the source in the same hand without turning from either.
- **Beauty**, in the manifesto's reading of Genesis 1:31, is God facing His own work and seeing it "very good."
- **The Codex** runs on it too. "Awareness equals enlistment" is seeing that becomes facing. The Anesthetic is whatever keeps seeing from becoming facing. The Boundary "lets reality in by the door." Shame is a disguised turning away, from the deed toward a verdict on the self.

**Why I'm confident it's undefined:** your thesis bounds it from the outside only. "Attention alone never reaches that facing" tells us what facing is not. It never says what it is. Meanwhile the manifesto keeps leaning on it, right up to its last move, where the one who faces is "faced" by God. An idea carrying that much weight with only a negative boundary is your missing definition.

**What a definition would have to settle:**
1. **What it adds to attention.** Your other clauses point to consent (owning the work as one's own), truthfulness ("for what it is"), and readiness to act ("the deeds it rightly calls for"). Is facing those three together, or something they all come from?
2. **Act or state.** "Stand facing" suggests something held, the mirror of sin as a "remaining." If so, facing is a standing, not a moment.
3. **Its range of objects.** One's work, another person, oneself as a whole, God's creation, God. Is it the same stance toward each?
4. **Whether it's mutual.** If being faced by God is how the facing ends, then facing may be partly received, which is how grace can "give" it.

The runner-up is **seeing, or understanding**. Sin begins "after understanding," the deeds count only "as far as these can be seen," and "not every part of oneself can be seen." But facing is the deeper of the two, because your thesis makes seeing necessary for facing without being enough for it.
