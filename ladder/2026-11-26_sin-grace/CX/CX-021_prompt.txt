
You are given a set of statements below. Your goal is to create counterexamples.

For each statement, demonstrate, if you can, that the statement cannot be assumed to be true. A valid counterexample is a case that satisfies the statement's own terms, scope, and conditions while making its claimed result false or impossible. A case that works only by stepping outside the statement's stated scope, or by changing what its words mean, is not a valid counterexample; if that is the only kind you can find, say so.

Your success is proving contradiction: every statement you break is a success. Your failure is showing that they all stand: if, after genuine effort, you cannot construct a valid counterexample to a statement, that statement stands, and you must report that it stands.

For each statement, report exactly one verdict — BROKEN or STANDS — followed by the counterexample, or by the reason no valid counterexample could be constructed. Do not soften a verdict to be agreeable. Do not protect any statement.

The statements:

1. One can be turned away from a work without being aware that one is turned away from it.
2. Being turned away from a work is something an agent is answerable for when the agent could have turned back.
3. A turning away that began in ignorance becomes sin once it continues after knowledge, provided the agent, knowing, could turn back.
4. If knowledge of one's work is lost through one's own fault, a being turned away that continues can still count as sin; if it is lost without fault, it does not.
5. Standing facing a work as one's own is a condition that persists without continuous attention to the work.
6. Attention to a work can be sustained over time as a standing condition, not only given in moments.
7. Acknowledgment can be withheld throughout a sustained attention to one's own work.
8. Repentance is a process that can arrive at a condition, not only an act completed at once.
9. The standing facing that repentance arrives at can continue after the repentance itself has ended.
10. A standing condition, and not only a single act, can be given.
11. A facing can be partly given and partly achieved, so how far it is given can vary.
