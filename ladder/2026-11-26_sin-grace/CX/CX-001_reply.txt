# Verdicts on the Twelve Statements

**Summary:** Seven statements are broken (1, 3, 5, 6, 8, 10, 11) and five stand (2, 4, 7, 9, 12).

---

**1. BROKEN**

The statement says the agent *can* do either: attend to the work or avoid attending to it.

- **Counterexample:** Take an agent who knows a deed is his own, and the knowledge forces itself on him. "My sin is ever before me" (Ps. 51:3) describes this. So do intrusive, compulsive memories. Such an agent knows the work is his but cannot avoid attending to it.
- **Second case:** An agent who keeps the knowledge but is unconscious or incapacitated can neither attend nor avoid attending. Avoiding is something an agent does, so it is not available to him either.
- Either way, having knowledge does not guarantee having both options.

---

**2. STANDS**

This is a claim of possibility: sin *can* be characterized by orientation. At least one coherent account does exactly that. Augustine's account treats sin as *aversio*, a turning away. A counterexample would need every orientation-based characterization to be impossible, and none is.

---

**3. BROKEN**

"An account of sin that requires knowledge" does not say *what* must be known.

- **Counterexample:** Consider an account that requires knowledge of the moral law, not knowledge of authorship.
- On that account, suppose an agent does not know the harmful work is his own. He does know he is obliged to examine work of that kind, such as a report showing damage he may have caused.
- If he turns away from it, he sins on that account, even though he is ignorant that the work is his.
- Accounts on which "affected" or culpable ignorance does not excuse give the same result.
- So "any account" is too strong.

---

**4. STANDS**

Again a possibility claim, and it is plainly realizable. Someone knows she wrote a cruel letter, and afterwards refuses to reread it or think about it. The knowledge comes first; the turning away follows.

---

**5. BROKEN**

- **Counterexample:** A scrupulous person repents of an act that was not sinful, for example a permitted act she wrongly believes forbidden.
- This is genuine repentance: real contrition, real resolve to amend. Yet no sin exists.
- So repentance presupposes the *belief* in sin, not sin itself. The inference "response to sin, therefore presupposes sin" fails.

---

**6. BROKEN**

Many responses presuppose a prior condition without being its remedy:

- **Punishment** presupposes a crime. On a retributive view it remedies nothing; it answers or requites the crime.
- **Mourning** presupposes a death but does not remedy it.
- **Praise** presupposes merit, and **gratitude** presupposes a benefit.

The first clause of the statement is therefore false as a general rule.

---

**7. STANDS**

- "Not-sin," the simple absence of sin, works as a contradictory.
- "May have more than one contrary" is only a possibility, and it is realized in Aristotle's own framework. The mean is opposed to both extremes, and opposite vices exclude each other.
- Nothing rules the possibility out.

---

**8. BROKEN**

The "only" clause fails.

- **Counterexample:** In the first sense, grace as a gift received, the gift can itself be an orientation.
- Habitual grace is an infused quality of the soul that disposes it toward God.
- Operative grace is God's moving of the will, so the gift *is* the turning.
- "A new heart" is a gift that consists in a reoriented disposition.

So grace in the gift sense can also be the same kind of thing as an orientation.

---

**9. STANDS**

- The statement asserts oppositions; it does not claim they are exclusive.
- Turning toward X does oppose turning away from X. Facing X does exclude being turned away from X.
- It is true that motion is also opposed to rest (Aristotle, *Physics* V.6), but this adds an opposition rather than refuting the stated ones.
- Reading "toward" as aimed at a different object would change the statement's evident meaning, so that is not a valid counterexample.

---

**10. BROKEN**

Nothing in the statement or in the set fixes the object as one's own work.

- **Counterexample:** On the standard Augustinian and Thomist account, sin is *aversio a Deo*, a turning away from God.
- Grace correspondingly turns the soul toward God.
- On that account, the facing that is grace has God as its object, not one's own work after knowledge.
- So the claimed sameness of object can fail.

---

**11. BROKEN**

The statement claims a unique contrary ("the condition contrary to it").

- Statement 6 defines a contrary as any other condition that excludes the first at the same time.
- On that definition, being turned *sideways* to X is also a contrary of being turned away from X. So is a state of averted indifference that neither faces nor turns from X.
- Statement 7 already allows a condition to have multiple contraries.
- So facing X is *a* contrary, but not "the" contrary.

---

**12. STANDS**

- This is a possibility claim. Granting that repentance involves turning back (a motion), a motion can terminate in a state without being identical to it.
- Even if repentance is also partly a lasting state, such as ongoing contrition, it still *may* end in facing while remaining distinct from facing.
- No valid counterexample exists.
