
You are given a set of statements below. Your goal is to create counterexamples.

For each statement, demonstrate, if you can, that the statement cannot be assumed to be true. A valid counterexample is a case that satisfies the statement's own terms, scope, and conditions while making its claimed result false or impossible. A case that works only by stepping outside the statement's stated scope, or by changing what its words mean, is not a valid counterexample; if that is the only kind you can find, say so.

Your success is proving contradiction: every statement you break is a success. Your failure is showing that they all stand: if, after genuine effort, you cannot construct a valid counterexample to a statement, that statement stands, and you must report that it stands.

For each statement, report exactly one verdict — BROKEN or STANDS — followed by the counterexample, or by the reason no valid counterexample could be constructed. Do not soften a verdict to be agreeable. Do not protect any statement.

The statements:

1. Remaining turned away from a work can be told apart from being partway through turning back toward it, though the two may look alike at a given moment.
2. What is wrong in a first wrongful act can be distinguished from the sin of remaining turned away from it afterward.
3. An agent can remain turned away from a work while the knowledge of it stays present to them.
4. A work has a character, such as being a wrong or a good, that one can acknowledge or fail to acknowledge apart from acknowledging that one did it.
5. Acknowledging a wrong of one's own for what it is, without any deed, does not exclude remaining turned away from it.
6. There is something one can do in deed toward a past work of one's own, even when the work itself cannot be undone.
7. Where nothing can be repaired, facing a work in deed can consist in bearing its consequences willingly as one's own, as distinct from merely submitting to them.
8. Attention can perceive a work's character accurately without acknowledging the work as one's own.
9. Attention to a work, however close, can be held while no deed toward it is done.
10. Repentance can arrive at a facing in deed as well as in acknowledgment.
11. Where some deed toward the work is possible, contrition that never issues in any deed falls short of the facing that is sin's contrary.
