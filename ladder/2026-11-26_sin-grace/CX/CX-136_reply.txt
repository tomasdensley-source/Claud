**Verdict: STANDS**

**1. The setup.** Repentance toward God is a return toward God (definition 4). So whatever stops a return from being fulfilled also stops repentance from being fulfilled. Definition 5 supplies that condition: a return is unfulfilled while there is something God made, which the returner can see, that he refuses to face both for what it is and as His workmanship.

**2. First branch of the conclusion: refusing to face what God made as His workmanship.**
- Definition 3 says facing a thing "as His workmanship" means facing it *also* that way, on top of facing it for what it is.
- So someone who refuses that kind of facing is refusing the very combination definition 5 requires.
- The conclusion limits itself to "as far as these can be seen," which matches definition 5's "which the one returning can see."
- So, by definition 5, this branch leaves repentance unfulfilled.

**3. Second branch: refusing to turn toward oneself within creation as a whole.**
- By definition 2, oneself is among what God made, and one *can* turn toward oneself as a whole. So the facing in question is possible, and oneself falls within definition 5's scope.
- "Oneself as a whole" is oneself as God made it together with what one has made of it. That is what oneself actually is.
- So refusing to face oneself as a whole is refusing to face this God-made thing for what it is. That already fails the first half of definition 5's combined facing.
- Definition 2 admits that not every part of oneself can be seen. The conclusion's "as far as these can be seen" mirrors definition 5's restriction to what can be seen, so no unseen part is being demanded.

**4. Attempted counterexamples, and why each fails.**
- **Facing only the God-made part of oneself.** Someone might face what God made in them while turning from what they have made of it, and claim to have met definition 5. This fails: they have not faced the thing for what it is, which includes what they made of it.
- **Repentance "found."** One might read "found" as already fulfilled, making the claim contradictory. In fact, if repentance is found and fulfilled, the condition "while it still refuses" does not hold, so the statement is simply not triggered. If "found" does not mean fulfilled, the argument above applies unchanged. Either way, no contradiction arises.
- **A brief facing.** Definition 1 allows facing "for a moment or as a standing." Could a momentary facing during an ongoing refusal satisfy definition 5? No: definition 5 and the conclusion both use the same "while … refuses" condition. The question is whether a refusal persists, not whether some facing occurred at some moment.

**5. Conclusion.** Every case I could build that keeps to the statement's own terms makes the conclusion follow from definitions 2 through 5. The only apparent counterexamples depend on redefining "for what it is" or "found," which steps outside the statement's terms. The statement stands.
