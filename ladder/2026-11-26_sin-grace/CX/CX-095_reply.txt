**Verdict: STANDS**

I could not build a counterexample that stays inside the statement's own terms. Most of it is stipulative definition: it fixes what "sin," "sin's contrary," "grace," and "repentance" mean. Contradicting those definitions from ordinary or traditional usage would mean changing what the words mean, which the rules exclude. A valid break would need an internal inconsistency, or a case that meets every stated condition while the stated result fails. These are the attempts I made and why each fails.

1. **Disowning a good deed.** Suppose someone understands they did a good act but disowns it out of humility ("it wasn't me"). By the definition, that is sin. That looks counterintuitive, but the statement anticipates it: the contrary is to stand toward the work "for what it is, right or wrong." Turning away from one's good work is meant to count. So this is a consequence of the definition, not a contradiction of it.

2. **Seeing oneself "as a whole" when not every part can be seen.** Fulfilled repentance requires turning toward oneself "as a whole, though not every part of oneself can be seen." This would only be contradictory if turning toward a whole required seeing all its parts. The statement denies that requirement. It also limits the earlier standing to what "can be seen." One can affirm or own a whole without inspecting every part, so the clauses are consistent.

3. **Grace as a gift versus repentance as an effort.** If the standing is freely given, can repentance reach it? Yes. Repentance is defined as turning toward that grace, and it contains the standing only "once it comes to" it. That is the reception of a gift, not self-production of it. The statement never claims the standing can be reached any other way, so nothing conflicts.

4. **Holding the standing without fulfilled repentance.** Someone might hold sin's contrary without having turned toward creation as God's work. The statement allows this: repentance can contain the contrary yet remain unfulfilled. The two thresholds are distinct but not contradictory.

5. **Dual authorship.** One's works are "one's own," yet one must turn toward creation, which includes oneself and one's deeds, as "God's work." This would contradict only if authorship had to be exclusive. The statement asserts no exclusivity, so dual or layered authorship is consistent with it.

6. **People who never understand their work.** Such a person is neither in sin nor in its contrary. The definitions are not required to sort everyone, so this is a gap in coverage, not a contradiction.

7. **Attention without deeds.** Mere attention without acknowledgment or the called-for deeds is explicitly excluded ("attention alone never amounts to that standing"). It therefore cannot serve as a counterexample case of the standing.

Every case I tried either fits the statement's own qualifications or depends on redefining its terms. The statement stands.
