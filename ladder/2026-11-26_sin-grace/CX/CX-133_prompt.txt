
You are given a set of statements below. Your goal is to create counterexamples.

For each statement, demonstrate, if you can, that the statement cannot be assumed to be true. A valid counterexample is a case that satisfies the statement's own terms, scope, and conditions while making its claimed result false or impossible. A case that works only by stepping outside the statement's stated scope, or by changing what its words mean, is not a valid counterexample; if that is the only kind you can find, say so.

Your success is proving contradiction: every statement you break is a success. Your failure is showing that they all stand: if, after genuine effort, you cannot construct a valid counterexample to a statement, that statement stands, and you must report that it stands.

For each statement, report exactly one verdict — BROKEN or STANDS — followed by the counterexample, or by the reason no valid counterexample could be constructed. Do not soften a verdict to be agreeable. Do not protect any statement.

The statements:

1. Where sin is remaining turned away, after understanding, from one's own work, and one's own work includes what one has left undone when it was one's to do, a man who understands a task God has called him to, flees it and holds to that flight, as Jonah fled toward Tarshish, both turns from God's call and sins.
2. Where sin is remaining turned away, after understanding, from one's own work, a man can turn from God's work without sinning: one who spends forty years at war with the body, century and temperament he was given turns from what made him, not from anything he made.
3. Where a return toward God is not fulfilled while the one returning still refuses to face something God has made that he can see, for what it is and as God's work, a man can stand turned toward every work of his own, in acknowledgment and in the deeds each calls for, while his repentance toward God remains unfulfilled, because he still refuses to face some of those around him as God's work.
4. Where a return toward God is not fulfilled while the one returning still refuses to face something God has made that he can see, for what it is and as God's work, a return that was fulfilled can cease to be fulfilled, if he later comes to see something God has made and refuses to face it.
