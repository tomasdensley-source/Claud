**Statement 1: STANDS**

The statement does two things:

1. It distinguishes two ways of facing what God made:
   - (a) facing it for what it is, or
   - (b) facing it for what it is *and also* as God's work.
2. It claims that only (b) faces it as creation.

The word "also" fixes the scope. Mode (a) is defined as facing the thing without the reference to God's making. A valid counterexample would need someone who faces the thing as creation while in mode (a), that is, without facing it as God's work. Each route I tried fails.

- **Facing it "for what it is" already includes its being created.** One could argue that a creature's being simply *is* its createdness, so honest facing in mode (a) already faces it as creation. This rejects the statement's distinction rather than satisfying its terms. The "also" defines (a) as lacking the God's-work reference. If (a) secretly contains that reference, the case has become (b). It does not break the statement; it changes what "for what it is" means.

- **Facing it as made, but by someone other than God.** Examples are a Gnostic demiurge, or "creation" as the product of evolution or nature. Here the thing is faced as *a* product, but not as *God's* creation. In the statement, "creation" means the theological term for what God made. Treating some other making as "creation" shifts the word's meaning. Under the statement's own sense, this person has not faced it as creation.

- **Implicit or unconscious facing.** Suppose someone reverently attends to the world without explicitly thinking of God. If this counts as facing it as God's work implicitly, the case falls under (b). If it does not, the case falls under (a) and is not facing it as creation. Either way the claim holds.

- **Pure attention to the thing.** Consider attention that sets aside even the question of origin. Perhaps this lets the thing be "creation" more fully than mode (b). But "letting it be creation" is a claim about the thing's status, not about the observer facing it as creation. Making the case work requires equivocating on "faces it as creation."

**Why no counterexample exists:** In the statement's sense, "creation" means "what God made, as God's work." So facing something *as creation* just is facing it as God's work. That is mode (b) by definition. The claim is close to analytic given its own terms. Every apparent counterexample either collapses into (b) or redefines "creation" or "for what it is."
