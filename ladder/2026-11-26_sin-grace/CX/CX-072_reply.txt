# Counterexamples to the Two Statements on Repentance

## 1. "Facing God's creation is necessary for repentance to be taking place."

**Verdict: STANDS**

**Why no valid counterexample could be built:** I tried several candidates. Each one either still faces part of creation or leaves the statement's scope.

- **A hermit repenting an inward sin, such as pride, before God alone.** He never looks at the outside world. But he must face his own pride, and he is himself part of God's creation, as Statement 2 makes explicit. So he still faces creation.
- **A sin committed directly against God, such as blasphemy.** Repenting still requires the creature to face his own act and guilt.
- **A general confession ("I repent of all my sins, known and unknown").** Even this minimal repentance requires recognising oneself as a sinner, which is facing a created being: oneself.
- **The thief on the cross, who repented in a moment.** He still faces his own guilt: "we indeed justly; for we receive the due reward of our deeds" (Luke 23:41).
- **An atheist "repenting."** This leaves the statement's theistic frame, so it is invalid.
- **Repentance with no awareness of wrongdoing at all.** This changes what "repentance" means, since repentance is a turning from known sin. It is also invalid.

Repentance is a creature turning from its own wrongdoing. The repenter and the wrongdoing are always part of creation. So within the statement's terms, repentance cannot occur without some facing of creation.

## 2. "Because one is oneself part of God's creation, repentance is not fulfilled until one looks inward and faces oneself wholly as part of God's creation."

**Verdict: BROKEN**

**Counterexample:** Take the penitent of Psalm 19:12: "Who can understand his errors? Cleanse thou me from secret faults."

- He faces the sins he can see.
- He openly admits that parts of himself remain hidden from him.
- He entrusts those hidden parts to God.

He has not faced himself *wholly*. In the same framework the statement assumes, the human heart is not fully knowable even to itself (Jeremiah 17:9). Yet his repentance is accepted and complete. Psalm 32:5 describes the same pattern: "I acknowledged my sin… and thou forgavest."

The thief on the cross makes the point sharper. He had minutes, not a lifetime of introspection. Yet he received "Today shalt thou be with me in paradise" (Luke 23:43). Fulfilment of repentance hardly gets more explicit than that, and it came without any whole self-examination.

**Two ways the statement fails:**

- **The inference does not follow.** Being part of creation, together with Statement 1's requirement to face creation, only shows that one must face *some* part of creation. It does not show that one must face *all* of oneself.
- **Total self-facing is impossible for finite humans.** If it were required, no one's repentance would ever be fulfilled. That contradicts the framework's own clear cases of fulfilled repentance.
