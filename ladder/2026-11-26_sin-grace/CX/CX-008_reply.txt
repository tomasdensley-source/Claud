## 1. STANDS

The usual ways to break a claim like this all fail, because the statement is hedged against each of them.

**Attempt: repentance that never reaches the facing.** Someone sins, feels remorse, confesses and does penance, but never actually faces the work of his hand. This would refute "repentance is the contrary of sin." The statement does not claim that, though. It says repentance is the contrary *only insofar as* it arrives at the facing. Repentance that falls short is simply not the contrary, which is what the statement allows for.

**Attempt: facing without repentance.** Someone who never turned away faces his work, or a sinner is brought to the facing directly. The statement never says repentance is the only path to the facing. The facing is defined on its own terms ("the facing of that same work under the same knowledge"). Grace is named separately as the facing when it is given rather than achieved, so the statement already allows facing to arrive without repentance.

**Attempt: facing that is partly given and partly achieved.** This would trouble a strict either/or. But the statement only says what grace is: the facing *when* given. It does not claim every facing must be purely one or the other.

**Attempt: repentance without prior sin.** The statement fixes repentance as what "comes after sin." A "repentance" with nothing to repent of would mean changing the word's meaning, which is outside the allowed scope.

Every condition is either stipulated or qualified, so nothing inside its terms makes its claims false.

## 2. BROKEN

The weak point is the unqualified clause "Repentance **is** the road back to that facing." It asserts two things: that repentance leads back to the facing, and, through the definite article, that it is *the* road.

**Counterexample A: repentance that leads away from the facing.**
- A man betrays a friend. He knew what he was doing, and the betrayal is the work of his own hand. Afterward he turns away from it. By the statement's own definition, this is sin.
- He then truly repents: deep contrition, confession, fasting, self-punishment.
- But all of this absorbs his attention in his own guilt, his penance and his standing. His contrition becomes a new way of not looking at the thing he did and its effect on his friend.
- This is real repentance in the ordinary sense, and it comes after sin as the statement requires. Yet it does not lead back to the facing. It extends the turning away by other means.
- So repentance is not, without qualification, the road back to the facing.

Statement 1 avoids this case with "only insofar as it arrives." Statement 2 drops that qualifier.

**Counterexample B: the facing reached by another way.**
- Statement 2 says grace is "the facing given."
- Take a sinner to whom the facing is simply given, with no prior repentance, which the definition of grace permits.
- That sinner has come back to the facing without walking the road of repentance.
- So repentance is not *the* road back. At most it is one road. The statement's own definition of grace contradicts the exclusivity of its claim about repentance.

Either case meets the statement's terms and falsifies its claim about repentance.
