
You are given a set of statements below. Your goal is to create counterexamples.

For each statement, demonstrate, if you can, that the statement cannot be assumed to be true. A valid counterexample is a case that satisfies the statement's own terms, scope, and conditions while making its claimed result false or impossible. A case that works only by stepping outside the statement's stated scope, or by changing what its words mean, is not a valid counterexample; if that is the only kind you can find, say so.

Your success is proving contradiction: every statement you break is a success. Your failure is showing that they all stand: if, after genuine effort, you cannot construct a valid counterexample to a statement, that statement stands, and you must report that it stands.

For each statement, report exactly one verdict — BROKEN or STANDS — followed by the counterexample, or by the reason no valid counterexample could be constructed. Do not soften a verdict to be agreeable. Do not protect any statement.

The statements:

1. To understand a work as one's own is to recognize which work is one's own and to grasp, as far as one can see, what it is and what it does; one who suspects a work is theirs and refuses to find out is not excused by that refusal.
2. To remain turned away from something is to hold a turning from it, rather than toward it, as a standing over time, and not to be in the course of turning back.
3. To stand turned toward something is to hold a turning toward it as a standing over time, which persists without continuous attention to it.
4. The contrary of a standing is a standing that cannot hold together with it in the same subject, toward the same object, at the same time, though both can be absent.
5. To hold a work as one's own is to acknowledge oneself as its author, inwardly or in speech, rather than to disown it or lay it on another.
6. To take a work for what it is is to take it by its character as far as one can see it, right or wrong, rather than by a flattering cover or by a condemnation of the person in place of the deed.
