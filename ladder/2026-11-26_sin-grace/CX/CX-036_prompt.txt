
You are given a set of statements below. Your goal is to create counterexamples.

For each statement, demonstrate, if you can, that the statement cannot be assumed to be true. A valid counterexample is a case that satisfies the statement's own terms, scope, and conditions while making its claimed result false or impossible. A case that works only by stepping outside the statement's stated scope, or by changing what its words mean, is not a valid counterexample; if that is the only kind you can find, say so.

Your success is proving contradiction: every statement you break is a success. Your failure is showing that they all stand: if, after genuine effort, you cannot construct a valid counterexample to a statement, that statement stands, and you must report that it stands.

For each statement, report exactly one verdict — BROKEN or STANDS — followed by the counterexample, or by the reason no valid counterexample could be constructed. Do not soften a verdict to be agreeable. Do not protect any statement.

The statements:

1. The work of one's own hand includes what one left undone as well as what one did.
2. One can remain turned away from a work one has never yet faced, not only from a work one faced and then turned from.
3. One can know that a work is one's own without knowing what it is, as when its consequences are hidden.
4. One can face a work for what it is even while part of what it is remains unknown to one.
5. A work can be faced for what it is whether it is a wrong or a good.
6. Where what can rightly be done toward a past work is genuinely unclear, facing the work can include seeking out what would be right to do.
7. Where several deeds can rightly be done, the facing requires at least one of them, not every one.
8. Even if acknowledgment is a kind of attention, namely attention to the work as one's own, it adds the owning that bare attention lacks.
9. Even where the rightful deed consists in attention, the facing still requires an acknowledgment beyond it.
10. Repentance can arrive at the facing and later lose it.
11. Sorrow and resolve can accompany the facing without being required for it.
12. Where grace is defined as a given facing rather than as a turning back, it can be given to one who was never turned away.
