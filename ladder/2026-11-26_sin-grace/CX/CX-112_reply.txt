**1. BROKEN**

The conclusion is that *repentance itself* can turn toward God's creation as God's work, and toward oneself within it as a whole. The definitions leave two gaps that a consistent model can exploit.

**Gap 1: the condition in (4) is never shown to be met.**
- Definition (3) says repentance is the turning toward the grace that gives a maker *"the standing toward his own work."*
- Definition (4) applies only *"where repentance is the turning toward the grace that freely gives the facing of one's own work."*
- Nothing in the definitions says that having a standing toward one's work is the same as facing it.

**Gap 2: even where (4) applies, it does not say what the conclusion says.**
- (4) says the facing *"follows from"* repentance *"as a necessary implication."*
- That makes the facing a consequence of repentance. It does not make the facing something repentance itself turns toward.
- The conclusion needs the second claim, not the first.

**Counterexample model:**
- Take "standing toward one's own work" to mean being placed as its answerable maker: owning it and being accountable for it. It does not include beholding or facing it.
- Repentance is exactly what (3) says it is: the turning toward that grace. Its only object is the grace.
- (1) holds. A person is among what God made, can turn toward himself as a whole, and cannot see every part of himself.
- (2) holds. Facing creation as God's work remains a real possibility, distinct from facing things merely for what they are.
- (4) holds, but it never applies. In this model repentance is never "the turning toward the grace that gives the *facing* of one's own work," so the condition in (4) is never met.
- Even in a variant where (4) does apply, the facing of creation arises as a consequence of repentance. Repentance's own object is still only grace.

**Result:** all four definitions are satisfied, yet repentance is not something that turns toward God's creation as God's work, or toward oneself within it. The claimed result does not follow. It would need two added assumptions:
- that "standing toward" one's work means "facing" it; and
- that what follows from repentance is something repentance itself turns toward.

The statement supplies neither.
