# Verdicts on the Four Statements

## 1. STANDS

**Checking the terms:**
- **The task is his.** A task God has called him to is, by that call, his to do.
- **He understands it.** The statement says so.
- **He leaves it undone.** By fleeing and holding to the flight, he leaves it undone while it is his.
- **It counts as his own work.** The inclusion clause makes the omitted task part of his own work.
- **He remains turned away.** Holding to the flight is remaining turned away, after understanding, from his own work.

That meets the given definition of sin exactly. Fleeing the task God called him to is also, plainly, turning from God's call.

**Attempted counterexamples:**
- **The task was not yet due.** This fails, because he already "holds to" the flight, so the task stands undone while it is his.
- **He did not grasp the call.** This contradicts the stipulation that he understands it.

No case within the statement's terms keeps the flight and escapes either conclusion.

## 2. STANDS

**Checking the terms:**
- **What sin is limited to.** Sin is defined as remaining turned away from one's *own* work.
- **What he rejects.** The body, century and temperament were given to him, not made by him. They are God's work.
- **The result.** A man can be turned against all three without being turned away from anything he made, so the definition does not count it as sin.

**Attempted counterexample:** The forty-year war might crowd out duties he owes, so he does leave his own work undone. This fails for two reasons:
- The claim is only that such a man *can* do this without sinning. One case where the war neglects no duty of his own is enough.
- Statement 2's condition does not even include the "left undone" clause from statement 1.

Calling the war itself "his own work" does not help either. He is not turned *away* from the war; he is fully engaged in it.

## 3. STANDS

**Checking the terms:**
- **The neighbours are God's work.** The people around him were made by God, and he can see them.
- **The refusal blocks fulfilment.** Refusing to face them as God's work leaves his return unfulfilled under the stated condition.
- **This is compatible with facing all his own works.** Suppose one of his works is a wrong he did to a neighbour. He can acknowledge it and make full restitution while still refusing to see that neighbour *as God's work*. Nothing in the condition makes that refusal a turning-away from his own work.

**Attempted counterexample:** The refusal is itself a duty he leaves undone. This would require importing statement 1's inclusion clause and treating "facing others as God's work" as his own work. That changes the terms rather than satisfying them. The claim is again only one of possibility, which the case above meets.

## 4. STANDS

**Checking the terms:**
- **Fulfilment is tied to time.** The condition says the return is "not fulfilled *while*" a refusal persists.
- **Later refusal breaks it.** If a man whose return was fulfilled later sees something God made and refuses to face it, the condition holds again, and his return is not fulfilled at that time.
- **So it can cease.** Nothing in the terms forbids such a later sight and refusal, so a fulfilled return can stop being fulfilled.

**Attempted counterexample:** A fulfilled return is a completed past achievement, so later refusal is a new turning-away rather than an undoing of the old return. This redefines "fulfilled" as a permanent event. The statement's own "while" treats fulfilment as a continuing state, so the objection changes what the word means.

---

**Result: no statement is broken. All four stand.**
