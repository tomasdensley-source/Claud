**1. BROKEN**

The conclusion makes two claims about repentance. It says repentance can turn toward God's creation as God's work, and that it can turn toward oneself within that creation as a whole. The definitions support neither claim.

**What the definitions establish:**
- Definition (1) says that *one* can turn toward oneself as a whole. It says nothing about repentance.
- Definition (2) distinguishes two ways of facing creation. It does not say what repentance faces.
- Definition (3) is the only definition of repentance. It identifies repentance with *turning toward grace*, specifically the grace that freely gives "a maker" standing toward his own work. The object of repentance is that grace. It is not the work, and it is not creation.

**Counterexample (a model satisfying all three definitions):**
- Let "a maker" in (3) be the human person. The grace in question gives the person standing toward what he has made of himself.
- Repentance is the person's turning toward that grace, and only that.
- Meanwhile, the person faces God's creation, including himself as made, only "for what it is," in the first sense of (2). He never faces it "also as God's work."
- The person can still turn toward himself as a whole under (1). But that turning is a capacity of the person, not an act of repentance.

Every definition holds in this model:
- The person is among what God made.
- He can turn toward himself as a whole, with some parts unseen.
- The two modes of facing creation are distinguished.
- Repentance is exactly the turning toward the grace that gives a maker standing toward his own work.

Yet repentance in this model has no orientation toward creation as God's work, and none toward oneself within it as a whole.

**Why this is not stepping outside the terms:**
- The conclusion depends on reading "a maker" in (3) as God, or on treating "turning toward the grace" as also turning toward the work that the grace concerns. Neither reading is required by the text.
- The phrase "a maker" is generic.
- Turning toward a grace is not the same as turning toward the work to which that grace gives standing.
- The capacity described in (1) is attributed to "one," not to repentance.

Since a model satisfying every definition exists in which the claimed result fails, the conclusion does not follow from the definitions.
