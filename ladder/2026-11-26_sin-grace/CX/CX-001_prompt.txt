
You are given a set of statements below. Your goal is to create counterexamples.

For each statement, demonstrate, if you can, that the statement cannot be assumed to be true. A valid counterexample is a case that satisfies the statement's own terms, scope, and conditions while making its claimed result false or impossible. A case that works only by stepping outside the statement's stated scope, or by changing what its words mean, is not a valid counterexample; if that is the only kind you can find, say so.

Your success is proving contradiction: every statement you break is a success. Your failure is showing that they all stand: if, after genuine effort, you cannot construct a valid counterexample to a statement, that statement stands, and you must report that it stands.

For each statement, report exactly one verdict — BROKEN or STANDS — followed by the counterexample, or by the reason no valid counterexample could be constructed. Do not soften a verdict to be agreeable. Do not protect any statement.

The statements:

1. An agent who knows a work to be their own can either attend to it or avoid attending to it.
2. Sin can be characterized by the agent's orientation toward a deed, not solely by the deed's transgression of a law.
3. On any account of sin that requires knowledge, turning away from one's own work in ignorance of it does not count as sin.
4. Knowledge of one's own work can be had before one turns away from it.
5. Repentance is a response to sin and so presupposes sin.
6. A response that presupposes a prior condition stands to that condition as its remedy, not as its contrary; the contrary of a condition is another condition that excludes it at the same time.
7. Sin has a contradictory, which is simply its absence, and it may have more than one contrary.
8. Grace can name a gift received or the receptive state of the one who receives it, and only in the second sense is it the same kind of thing as an orientation.
9. The motion of turning away is opposed by turning toward, while the state of being turned away is opposed by facing.
10. The facing that is grace has the same object as the turning away that is sin: one's own work, after knowledge.
11. Where a condition is defined as being turned away from an object, the condition contrary to it is facing that same object.
12. Turning back is a motion and facing is a state, so repentance may end in facing without being the same as facing.
