1. **DEMONSTRATED.** Neglect and drift away from a task are commonly noticed only afterward, so the turning away came before any awareness of it.

2. **CONTESTED.** Whether one answers for a turning away depends on whether it was avoidable, and views differ on whether turning that happens without awareness counts.

3. **CONTESTED.** It holds only if knowing also brings the ability to turn back and the obligation to do so, and that is disputed.

4. **CONTESTED.** Culpable forgetting may keep blame in place, but innocent loss of knowledge seems to restore the original ignorance, so the claim holds only in some cases.

5. **DEMONSTRATED.** Commitments and ownership hold through sleep and distraction, so standing facing a work does not need attention at every moment.

6. **FALSE AS STATED.** If one can be turned away without awareness (as item 1 shows), the turning away cannot lie wholly in acknowledgment, so conduct can break the facing.

7. **DEMONSTRATED.** Sustained vigilance over long stretches, such as a watchman's or a craftsman's, is an ordinary and well-documented form of attention, not only a string of moments.

8. **DEMONSTRATED.** One can labor attentively at a work while refusing to own it, as with coerced or disavowed work.

9. **DEMONSTRATED.** Repentance often unfolds over time through stages such as recognition, contrition and amendment, and ends in a settled state.

10. **DEMONSTRATED.** Like any result that outlasts the process that produced it, and given item 5, the facing can persist once the repenting is complete.

11. **DEMONSTRATED.** Statuses, trust and permissions are conferred as ongoing conditions, not single acts.

12. **CONTESTED.** Facing could be treated as all-or-nothing, like acknowledgment, or as graded, like attention, and the propositions do not settle which.
