**1. DEMONSTRATED.** An omission one could and should have performed is attributable to the agent just as an act is, so it belongs to what one's hand has wrought.

**2. DEMONSTRATED.** "Remaining turned away" names a continuing state, and that state can hold from the first moment of knowledge without any prior facing.

**3. CONTESTED.** It is unsettled whether knowing only *that* a work is one's own suffices for culpable aversion, or whether knowing *what* it is is also required.

**4. DEMONSTRATED.** One can know one spoke a word or made a choice while its harmful consequences remain unseen.

**5. DEMONSTRATED.** If complete knowledge were required, no finite agent could face any work, since a work's consequences ramify without limit; facing it for what it is as far as known must suffice.

**6. DEMONSTRATED.** One can disown or flee one's good works through false humility, fear, or evasion of responsibility, so facing applies to goods as well as wrongs.

**7. FALSE AS STATED.** Taken universally, it fails: some works are irreparable or leave the right response genuinely unclear, as when the person wronged is dead.

**8. DEMONSTRATED.** Requiring every permissible deed could demand incompatible actions, so the obligation must be disjunctive.

**9. CONTESTED.** Attention can clearly occur without acknowledgment, but whether acknowledgment is itself a special kind of attention, attention to the work *as one's own*, remains open.

**10. DEMONSTRATED.** Even when the rightful deed is attention, such as attending to the one harmed, that is distinct from owning the work, so acknowledgment is still required in addition.

**11. DEMONSTRATED.** Facing is a sustained stance rather than a single event, and relapse into aversion after genuine turning is possible and common.

**12. CONTESTED.** Whether sorrow and resolve belong to the contrary of sin or are only its accompaniments depends on disputed accounts of what that contrary is.

**13. CONTESTED.** The compatibility of a received facing with the agent's ownership of it is the long-disputed question of grace and freedom.

**14. CONTESTED.** If grace names only a turning back, it presupposes prior aversion; if it can be given at the first moment of knowledge, it need not.
