# P01 Claims extraction

Extractor: the operator (this session). Construction, untested.
Delimitation criterion used (the skill leaves it OPEN, §6 P1): one claim per assertion the sentence makes, including the inferential "therefore" as its own claim.
Workflow status of every item: Extracted.

### S-001
Text: Sin is the turning away from the work of one's own hand.
Anchor: "Sin is the turning away, ... from the work of one's own hand"
- S-001.a: A person's own work remains present to them as something they can face or turn away from.
- S-001.b: Sin is essentially an orientation toward something, not an act of transgression against a law.

### S-002
Text: The turning away that is sin happens after knowledge.
Anchor: "after knowledge"
- S-002.a: Turning away from one's own work without knowledge of it is not sin.
- S-002.b: Knowledge of one's own work can be had before one turns away from it.

### S-003
Text: The opposite of sin is not repentance.
Anchor: "its opposite is not repentance"
- S-003.a: Repentance is a response to sin and so presupposes sin.
- S-003.b: What presupposes a thing cannot be that thing's opposite.

### S-004
Text: The opposite of sin is grace.
Anchor: "but grace"
- S-004.a: Sin has exactly one opposite.
- S-004.b: Grace and sin are of the same logical kind, so one can be the opposite of the other.

### S-005
Text: Grace is the facing.
Anchor: "grace, which is the facing"
- S-005.a: The opposite of turning away is facing.
- S-005.b: The facing that is grace has the same object as the turning away that is sin: the work of one's own hand, after knowledge.

### S-006
Text: The definition of sin in S-001 and S-002 entails S-003, S-004 and S-005.
Anchor: "therefore"
- S-006.a: The opposite of a defined thing is found by inverting its defining relation.
- S-006.b: Repentance (turning back) and facing are distinct orientations.
